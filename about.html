About

From farm plots to data plots.

I started my career in an agricultural classroom in Nigeria. Today I use data and research to understand how college students — especially in STEM — find their footing in a world being reshaped by AI.

My path

  1. Nigeria

    Teaching agriculture

    I learned to teach in agricultural classrooms, where the lesson was always the same: outcomes depend on conditions. Soil, water, timing and care shape the harvest — just as advising, course design and belonging shape a student’s path. [Add school/years]

  2. Graduate study

    Moving into education research

    Curiosity about why some learners thrive and others don’t led me to graduate study in education, and to the quantitative methods I now use every day. [Add degree/program]

  3. Higher education data

    Institutional data & analytics

    I build dashboards and analyses that help colleges see student success more clearly — from first-year retention to career outcomes — in R, Python and Power BI.

  4. Now

    Research on AI & student success

    My developing dissertation work asks how generative AI is changing what success looks like for STEM undergraduates, and for whom.

What I care about

I’m interested in questions where data and people meet: Which students get to experiment with new tools, and which are left guessing at hidden rules? What does a retention rate tell us about the conditions we create? How can institutions use analytics without reducing students to risk scores?

I write about these questions on the blog, one theme for each day of the week.
